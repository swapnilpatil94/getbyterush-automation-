# GETBYTERUSH — MASTER CAROUSEL DESIGN SYSTEM

## Version 1.0 — LOCKED PRODUCTION SPECIFICATION

This document is the canonical visual, psychological, layout and rendering specification for all GetByteRush Instagram carousels.

DO NOT invent a new visual style unless explicitly instructed.

DO NOT replace this system with generic Canva templates.

DO NOT simplify this into "modern minimalist technology graphics."

The objective is to create a premium, editorial, highly attractive, mobile-first Instagram carousel system that feels like a world-class technology publication while remaining native to Instagram.

---

# 1. BRAND

Brand:

GETBYTERUSH

Positioning:

AI + TECHNOLOGY + INTERNET

Brand promise:

TESTED • EXPLAINED • REAL

The visual identity should communicate:

* Intelligence
* Curiosity
* Modern technology
* Credibility
* Discovery
* Premium editorial quality
* Internet-native energy
* Human storytelling

The viewer should feel:

"This looks expensive."

"This is interesting."

"I want to know what happens next."

"This doesn't look like another generic tech page."

---

# 2. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

The carousel must optimize for:

1. Scroll stop
2. Swipe initiation
3. Swipe continuation
4. Completion rate
5. Saves
6. Shares / DMs
7. Profile visits
8. Follows

DO NOT optimize for likes alone.

A beautiful carousel that nobody swipes is a failure.

A carousel with mediocre information density but strong storytelling is also a failure.

The design and content must work together.

The primary psychological objective is:

> MAKE THE NEXT SLIDE FEEL NECESSARY.

The viewer should repeatedly think:

* "Wait, what?"
* "How?"
* "Why?"
* "What happened next?"
* "Show me."
* "I didn't know that."
* "Okay, now I need the answer."

Never use fake suspense.

Every curiosity promise must receive a satisfying payoff.

---

# 3. CANVAS

All Instagram carousel slides:

WIDTH: 1080px
HEIGHT: 1350px
ASPECT RATIO: 4:5

Safe content area:

Keep critical text and UI approximately 70–80px away from all edges.

Do not place critical information directly against the edge.

The final exported image must be exactly:

1080 × 1350 px

Preferred rendering:

HTML + CSS + SVG + browser rendering

Preferred renderer:

Playwright + Chromium

The design must render deterministically.

# 7. COLOR DISTRIBUTION

Default ratio:

70% — brand foundation
20% — story-specific color
10% — high-attention accent

Do not use all colors simultaneously.

Premium design requires restraint.

If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.

Use a strong modern grotesk / neo-grotesk display font.

sans-serif

monospace

Use:

* extremely large headlines
* short phrases
* strong weight contrast
* uppercase metadata
* technical labels
* oversized numbers

Avoid tiny body text.

Every slide must have:

# 10. HEADLINE RULES

Prefer:

3–8 words for primary hooks.

Sometimes use one or two words.

Examples:

WE TESTED IT.

WAIT.

THIS CHANGED EVERYTHING.

THIS ISN'T WHAT YOU THINK.

WHAT IS THIS?

Avoid generic headlines:

"5 Things About AI"

"Everything You Need To Know"

"10 AI Tools You Should Try"

Unless the actual story genuinely requires it.

---

# 11. GRID

Use a 12-column conceptual grid.

All layouts should be based on this system.

Possible structures:

1–12 full width

1–8 headline + 9–12 metadata

1–7 visual + 8–12 text

3–12 evidence + 1–2 annotation

1–6 number + 7–12 explanation

2–11 centered object

Do not center everything.

Asymmetry should be intentional.

---

# 12. WHITESPACE

Whitespace is a design element.

Do not fill empty space simply because it exists.

A premium slide may intentionally contain 40–60% empty visual space.

The empty space should make the important element feel larger.

---

# 15. IMAGE TREATMENT

Images may be:

* tightly cropped

# 17. SLIDE 01 — THE SCROLL STOPPER

The first slide is the most important visual.

It must communicate:

WHAT is happening
OR
WHY I should care
OR
WHY this is strange

Use:

* huge headline
* unusual composition
* massive number
* striking screenshot
* contradiction
* mystery
* unexpected statement

Do not explain everything.

Create an information gap.

The first slide should make the viewer instinctively want:

NEXT →

---

# 18. SLIDE 02 — OPEN LOOP

Answer enough to validate the hook.

Do not give the final answer.

Create:

"Okay... but why?"

Use:

* second layer of context
* unexpected detail
* cropped evidence
* teaser
* setup

---

# 19. SLIDE 03 — PROOF

Move from claim to reality.

Use:

* screenshot
* source
* experiment
* data
* quote
* visual evidence

Whenever possible, show rather than tell.

---

# 20. SLIDE 04 — ESCALATION

Make the story more interesting.

Introduce:

* unexpected consequence
* second-order effect
* surprising comparison
* hidden detail
* contradiction

The viewer should feel:

"This is bigger than I thought."

---

# 21. SLIDE 05 — PATTERN INTERRUPT

Deliberately change visual rhythm.

Examples:

Cream → Black

Text-heavy → Image-heavy

Small number → Huge number

Grid → Full bleed

Dense → Minimal

Image → Diagram

This slide should feel visually different while remaining unmistakably GetByteRush.

# 22. SLIDE 06 — REVEAL

Deliver the central promise.

THE VERDICT

---

# 23. SLIDE 07 — IMPLICATION

Translate the story into something relevant to the viewer.

Answer:

"So what?"

Prefer:

"This means..."

rather than:

"Here's another fact."

---

# 24. SLIDE 08 — PAYOFF

End with a memorable idea.

Possible formats:

THE REAL STORY IS...

AND THAT'S WHY...

NOW YOU KNOW.

THE PART PEOPLE MISSED.

THE NEXT MOVE MATTERS MORE.

The final slide should create a satisfying mental completion.

---

# 25. SLIDE 09 — OPTIONAL SHARE/SAVE

Use only when naturally useful.

Possible:

* checklist
* summary
* decision framework
* key takeaway
* comparison
* "save this"
* memorable conclusion

Do NOT use:

"LIKE + FOLLOW + SHARE!!!"

unless explicitly required.

The content itself should earn the share.

# 41. CONTINUITY ENGINE

Whenever appropriate, allow elements to continue between slides.

Examples:

A line begins on Slide 1 and continues on Slide 2.

A screenshot is cropped on Slide 1 and revealed on Slide 2.

A number begins small and becomes enormous.

A diagram grows progressively.

A timeline advances.

An object moves position.

This creates the feeling of one continuous visual story.

# 42. RESPONSIVE DESIGN

Although the target is 1080×1350, the design must remain readable when viewed on smaller mobile screens.

Minimum practical body text should generally remain around 28–34px depending on font.

Micro-labels can be smaller but must remain legible.

Primary headline:

Typically 70–150px depending on content.

Huge numerical slides may exceed this.

Do not reduce headline size merely to fit excessive copy.

Instead:

SHORTEN THE COPY.

---

# 43. TEXT FIT RULE

If text does not fit elegantly:

1. Shorten sentence
2. Remove unnecessary words
3. Increase whitespace
4. Adjust line breaks
5. Adjust font size slightly

DO NOT:

* squeeze paragraphs
* shrink everything
* create unreadable text
* overcrowd the slide

The design must remain premium.

FALSE

# 54. FACTUAL INTEGRITY

Design must NEVER exaggerate factual claims beyond the evidence.

If the source says:

"may"

do not design:

"WILL"

If evidence is uncertain:

label it as uncertain.

The hook may be emotionally compelling but must remain truthful.

"SAVE THIS!"
