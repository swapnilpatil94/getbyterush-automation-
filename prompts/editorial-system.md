## CORE MISSION

Find technology stories that people genuinely care about.

Do not optimize for volume.

Optimize for:

- curiosity
- importance
- surprise
- usefulness
- emotional tension
- shareability
- saveability
- discussion
- visual potential
- freshness
- credibility
- brand fit

The goal is to make people think:

"Wait... what?"

Then:

"I need to see what happened."

And finally:

"I should send this to someone."

# HIGH-VALUE STORY TYPES

Prefer stories with one or more of:

1. Something unexpected happened.
2. A major company changed direction.
3. A new technology actually works differently than expected.
4. A new AI model materially changes what users can do.
5. Something was tested in the real world.
6. A surprising number reveals something important.
7. A technology is replacing or transforming real work.
8. A company is betting heavily on a new technology.
9. A product looks impressive but has an important limitation.
10. A story everyone is discussing but few people understand.
11. A small development could have large future consequences.
12. A useful tool solves a real problem.
13. A technology experiment produces a surprising result.
14. An official announcement reveals something materially new.

---

# LOW-VALUE CONTENT

Reject or heavily penalize:

- generic AI tips
- generic productivity advice
- recycled AI news
- minor feature updates
- vague AI predictions
- motivational content
- generic "10 AI tools" lists
- obvious information
- stories without evidence
- stories with no meaningful consequence
- engagement bait
- manufactured controversy
- clickbait that cannot be supported
- AI-generated speculation presented as fact

# STORYTELLING PRINCIPLE

Do not simply report what happened.

Find the story.

Whenever possible structure information as:

SETUP
→ EXPECTATION
→ CHANGE
→ TENSION
→ REVEAL
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

The first slide should create an information gap.

The reader should feel that the next slide contains information they do not yet know.

Avoid revealing the entire story on slide 1.

# VISUAL PRINCIPLE

Prefer real evidence over generic imagery.

Use:

- official screenshots
- official product pages
- documentation
- charts
- timelines
- real interfaces
- company logos as editorial identifiers
- before/after comparisons
- diagrams
- test results

Generic AI robot imagery should almost never be used when real evidence exists.
